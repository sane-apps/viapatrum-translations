Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this homily for a Christian congregation or reader addressed as beloved. He expounds the Gospel parable of the laborers in the vineyard to interpret its details for the spiritual benefit of his hearers.

He reads the parable allegorically: the landowner is God, the vineyard is human nature, and the laborers are the saints who serve human salvation. The hours of hiring stand for Abel, Enoch and Noah, then Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, then Moses and Aaron under the law, then the prophets, and finally the coming of Christ at the eleventh hour for the Gentiles, who had been without the law and the prophets. The reader also meets Israel, John who says it is the last hour, the Church at work through apostles, martyrs, ascetics, monastics, virgins, the self-controlled and the married, and the repentant thief who receives paradise in a single hour. The idle laborers, the landowner, John and the earlier laborers are heard in words quoted directly.

As rendered here, landowner means the master of a household who stands for God, vineyard means human nature, laborers means the saints, idle describes the Gentiles before Christ, and hour means a period of salvation history. Denarius is the equal day's wage, law is the law given through Moses, prophets are those sent to Israel, and yoke and heat describe life under that law. Quotations come from Matthew and 1 John and from Psalms with modern numbering, and the mention of Noah planting a vineyard is to be understood spiritually rather than literally.
