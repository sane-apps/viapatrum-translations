Hermas (fl. c. 140) was a Christian in Rome. He tells us that the man who raised him sold him as a slave to a woman named Rhoda, and that in later years he had a wife, children and a business of his own. He wrote this book for the whole church, and above all for its leaders, to call them to repentance.

The book is a record of what Hermas was shown and told. First the Church herself appears to him as a woman, old and bent at the start and younger at each meeting, and shows him a tower being built on the water: the Church, rising from stones that are believers. Women who stand for virtues, such as faith and self-control, hold up the tower and later carry its stones. Then an angel dressed as a shepherd, the angel of repentance, comes to live in his house. He gives Hermas twelve commandments and a series of parables, and at the end shows him the building of the tower once more, on a mountain in Arcadia. Near the end he explains that the Spirit who spoke to Hermas in the form of the Church was the Son of God. One call runs through it all: turn to God now, with your whole heart, while the door of repentance is still open.

A few things help a first-time listener. Hermas calls the woman “Lady” and the Shepherd “Sir.” At first he takes her for the Sibyl. The double-minded are those who waver between trusting God and doubting him. The seal is baptism, the water in which a person receives the name of the Son of God. “The nations” means the Gentiles. A station is a day of fasting. A great and glorious angel, named once as Michael, has charge of God’s people. Thegri is the angel set over wild beasts, and a second shepherd, the angel of punishment, disciplines those who stray. Hermas is to tell a man named Maximus that trouble is coming. Clement is to send the book on to the churches in other cities, and Grapte is to teach the widows and orphans. Hermas also quotes a book outside the Bible called Eldad and Modat.
