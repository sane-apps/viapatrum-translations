Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis in Cyprus, born near Eleutheropolis in Palestine. He held that post for nearly forty years until his death at sea on the return from Constantinople, and was known as a defender of orthodoxy.

He gave this homily to a Christian congregation celebrating the feast of the Ascension. It explains why the Ascension completes and crowns the feasts of Christ, and answers Jewish objections that Elijah also ascended by arguing from Scripture that Elijah was taken only as into heaven while Christ who came down from heaven truly ascended.

The reader meets Christ whose flesh is exalted to heaven, Elijah held up against him, John who proclaims him at his baptism, the Father who receives the sheep, the devil whose first-person lament is quoted, the Jews whose objection is quoted and answered, and the two men in white who promise his return in the same way. Christ also speaks as the shepherd offering his gift to the Father, and verses from the Psalms are quoted in the lament and by the preacher. The parable of the sower is applied to the Church as good soil bearing fruit thirty, sixty and a hundredfold. Here coming down means God's becoming human, flesh means his human nature, and Epiphany means the feast of his baptism, while LXX marks the Septuagint, the ancient Greek Old Testament, whose Psalm numbers differ from the Hebrew.
