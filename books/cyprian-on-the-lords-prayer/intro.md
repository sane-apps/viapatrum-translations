Cyprian of Carthage (c. 200–258) was bishop of Carthage, trained in law and rhetoric before he became Christian, and wrote as pastor and teacher of his congregation.

He wrote this treatise for fellow Christians, his beloved brethren, to teach them how to pray and what the petitions of the Lord's Prayer mean for life. It was composed early in his time as bishop, after a long peace had weakened zeal and discipline in the African church, to give clear instruction in prayer and it follows the pattern of Tertullian's earlier Book on Prayer.

He presents the Lord's Prayer as Christ's own gift and explains each petition in turn, from praying our Father as children of God to seeking hallowed be your name, the kingdom of God, God's will, our daily bread understood as both the Eucharist and daily provision, forgiveness of debts linked to forgiving others, and protection in temptation and from evil through watchful prayer with unity, almsgiving and sacrifice. The reader meets Anna, the tax collector called the publican and the Pharisee, Abel and Cain, Daniel, Elijah, Job, Cornelius, Raphael with Tobit and Sarah, Peter, Paul, John, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Solomon, Nebuchadnezzar, Judas and Epaphroditus, and hears the words of Christ, Paul, John, Isaiah, Jeremiah and Raphael, with the proud prayer of the Pharisee set against the humble prayer of the publican. Old book names are given in modern form, such as the first of Kingdoms as 1 Samuel and Canticum canticorum as Song of Songs, Psalms follow the Septuagint numbering noted against the Hebrew numbering, and true sun means Christ.
