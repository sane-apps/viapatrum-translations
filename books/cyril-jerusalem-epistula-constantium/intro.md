Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 313–386) was the bishop of Jerusalem and the author of this letter.

He wrote to Constantius Augustus, the Roman emperor and son of Constantine, to report a luminous cross seen over Jerusalem during Pentecost, stretching from Golgotha to the Mount of Olives and visible to the whole city for hours. He wrote during Constantius conflict with the usurper Magnentius to declare his loyalty, to present the emperor as a Christian ruler favored by this heavenly vision, and to draw imperial attention to Jerusalem against its rival Caesarea.

Cyril reports the cross as brighter than the sun and links it to the sign of the Son of Man foretold in the Gospel according to Matthew, quoting Jesus speaking to his apostles and urging Constantius to study Matthew and take courage with God as ally before closing in prayer for the emperor and his family. Here sign means that foretold heavenly sign, cross means the trophy of Christ, heavenly vision means a sight granted from heaven, and piety means reverence toward God. Augustus means emperor, Pentecost is the fifty day feast after Easter, Golgotha is the place in Jerusalem where Christ was crucified, and the Mount of Olives is the mountain east of the city.
