Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was the bishop of Gabala in Syria. He wrote for Christian readers, especially those tempted to keep the law, to explain Paul's Letter to the Galatians and to counter the view that believers should live as Jews and keep the law.

This work survives only in fragments explaining that letter, written after the apostles had ruled against imposing the law while some Jewish Christians were still persuading believers to live as Jews. Severianus argues the law was a temporary bodily discipline that taught the mind to know sin but could not save or give life, while grace and faith in Christ bring freedom and baptism clothes all in Christ and makes them equal in new creation. Paul's own words from Galatians are quoted throughout, while the claims of those urging circumcision and law-keeping are argued against.

To follow it, law means commands about food, sabbath and circumcision, grace means God's gift of salvation in Christ, faith means faith in Christ by which people come to be justified, and flesh means bodily law-keeping and sinful desire. Allegory here does not leave history behind but means prophecy shown through events like Hagar and Sarah, elemental things means the weak old observances and pagan worship, promises means God's promises to Abraham fulfilled in Christ, and new creation replaces circumcision and uncircumcision. Quotations follow the Septuagint so Psalm numbers follow that Greek version, Sinai is used for Sina, and fl. c. 400 means active around 400.
