Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) was a Christian writer born in Ibora in Pontus who died in Cellia in the Nitrian Desert in Egypt. He was ordained by Basil of Caesarea and later served as archdeacon under Gregory of Nazianzus in Constantinople, where he took part in the Second Ecumenical Council. After a spiritual crisis he left for Jerusalem and joined the monastic communities of Melania the Elder and Rufinus of Aquileia, then spent the rest of his life studying, teaching and writing on the ascetic life in the monasteries of Nitria and Kellia.

He wrote this exhortation for monks to help them overcome the passions, cultivate virtues and hold right faith. It offers short proverbial admonitions on the life of virtue and combat with evil thoughts, grouped loosely around vices and purification and then trials, temptations and faithfulness to Trinitarian doctrine. Throughout it uses vivid comparisons from daily life and nature.

The argument is that each passion has its remedy in fasting, self-control with prayer, poverty, hope in God, reading, vigils, patience, tears, humility and stillness, and that the struggle of the Christian life requires effort, pure prayer and obedience to God's commandment. The Trinity one in being must be believed and worshipped rather than investigated, and neither faith and baptism without works of righteousness nor any tie of father, mother, wife, children or others excuses disobedience. The reader hears Evagrius speaking directly to monks, quoting the Psalms and other biblical language and answering an imagined objector who says he cannot keep the commandment. Here thought often means an evil thought, listlessness means the spiritual apathy of monks, and pure prayer is the weapon of that struggle, while Bible books use standard English names and Psalm numbers follow the Septuagint where it differs from the Hebrew.
