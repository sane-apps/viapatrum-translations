Gregory of Nazianzus (c. 329/330–390) delivered this homily to the congregation and people of Constantinople. He also addressed the people's passionate attachment to him after they had forcibly placed him on the throne, an act he describes as lawbreaking done in zeal and anger.

Gregory marvels at the people's love for him and his preaching, attributing it not to his own wisdom but to their prior faith and to God. He explains their affection as a response to his moderation, his suffering, and his defense of orthodox teaching. He denies desiring the see, presenting himself as a guardian and steward who will give the church away in marriage to another if he proves worthy of her beauty. He then exhorts the people to hold fast to the orthodox confession of the Trinity and to live virtuously, addressing various groups in the city.

To follow the work, a reader should know that the see means the episcopal throne of Constantinople, the orthodox faith means right doctrine concerning the Trinity, and the Godhead means the single divine nature shared by Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Gregory also speaks of the philosophical life, the confession of faith, the way of life, virtue, worship, and the purple robe of imperial authority.
