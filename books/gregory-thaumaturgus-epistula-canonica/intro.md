Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was the Christian bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus who wrote this letter to a holy pope, meaning another bishop. He writes to give pastoral guidance after barbarian invaders took Christians captive.

The invasion left Christians who ate foods placed before them by their captors and women who suffered violation by the barbarians. Gregory argues that such foods do not defile a person, quoting Paul the Apostle as introduced by 'the apostle says' and Jesus the Savior as introduced by 'the Savior... says.' He distinguishes women with an immoral disposition from women who had lived in chastity, and quotes Deuteronomy as introduced by 'we have an example in Deuteronomy' to show that a woman forced in the open country bears no capital sin. The reader meets Gregory and the holy pope, with these quoted voices, as Gregory directs how the captives may be restored to the prayers.

Here foods means food placed before the captives by the barbarians, captives means the Christians taken by the invaders, and defiles means makes common or unclean. Violation means forced sexual violation, chastity means a clean and sexually pure past life, immoral disposition means a settled habit of sexual immorality, capital sin means a sin deserving death, and prayers means the prayers of the church. Scripture is quoted from the Septuagint and the book is called Deuteronomy, with its wording noted where it differs from modern translations.
