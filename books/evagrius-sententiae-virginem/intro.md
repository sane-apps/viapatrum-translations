Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) was a Christian monk and former deacon and archdeacon. He was born in Ibora in Pontus, served in Constantinople, and later retreated to Jerusalem and then the Egyptian desert.

He wrote this exhortation for a consecrated virgin he addresses as child, to give practical ascetical guidance for life in a Christian community in guarding purity, humility and devotion to Christ as her heavenly bridegroom. It counsels her to love and serve Christ, honor her mother and sisters, pray constantly and practice self control, while avoiding encounters with men, anger, luxury, gossip, envy, slander, greed and lack of mercy that would extinguish her lamp. The reader meets Evagrius as spiritual father speaking in second person address with first person only in the close, the virgin herself, and Christ as Lord and bridegroom, and Evagrius quotes and answers imagined excuses that begin with Do not say.

To follow it a reader needs its central picture, in which the virgin dedicated to Christ keeps her lamp burning with the oil of mercy and good works as she waits for her bridegroom and for union with him in the bridal chamber. That picture comes from the Gospel parable of the ten virgins. Here freedom from passion means calm free from disordered desires, thoughts means tempting thoughts that defile the heart, mind means the understanding purified by vigil and prayer, and Trinity means the Holy Trinity to be remembered and worshipped.
