Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis in Cyprus. This work, known as Index of the Disciples, names no individual addressee. It records the names, offices and traditions about the deaths or later careers of the seventy disciples and the twelve apostles.

The catalogue first lists the seventy disciples, noting many as bishops of cities and how some died or fell away, then lists the twelve apostles with alternate names and family details, closing with James the brother of the Lord and the meaning of Jonah. Readers will meet James the Just, Stephen the first martyr, Timothy, Titus, Barnabas and Ananias, with Peter, Andrew, James, John, Matthew the tax collector and Thomas among the twelve. Paul and the apostle John are quoted in the entries.

To follow it, readers should know that bishop means overseer of a local church, deacon means one of the seven in Acts, apostle means one of the twelve or a commissioned messenger, and brother of the Lord means a relative of Jesus. Fall into heresy means to hold false doctrine, and sons of thunder gives the meaning of Boanerges. References to Acts, Luke, Matthew, Mark and Paul's letters to the Romans, Corinthians and Timothy use standard English Bible book names.
