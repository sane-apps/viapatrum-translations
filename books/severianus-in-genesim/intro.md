Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) wrote this homily for a Christian congregation. It is a sermon on Genesis 1:31. He defends the goodness of all created things, including animals used in Scripture as images of vice, and urges his hearers to value the divine words above all pleasures.

He argues that the virtues restore in us the original beauty of the image and likeness of God, while imitating the vices of irrational animals dishonors our rational freedom. The animals themselves are not evil by nature because God made all things good, and Scripture invites us to imitate their useful qualities, such as the diligence of the ant, the craftsmanship of the bee, the courage of the lion and the prudence of the serpent. The reader meets God the Creator, Christ the Savior, Jacob and his brother Esau, David the psalmist, Peter the apostle and the devil. Scripture is quoted directly with formulas such as Scripture says and David says, Christ is quoted speaking to the apostles, and Jacob is quoted speaking to Esau.

Here piety means reverent devotion to God, virtue means the path of likeness to God, and image of God and likeness refer to what is restored in humanity by virtue. Irrational animals means animals used as images of vice, excellent quality means a useful quality to imitate, prudent and innocent describe the serpent and the dove, diligence describes the ant and the bee, and honeycomb and divine words refer to Scripture. The Proverbist means the author of Proverbs. Modern citations follow standard English Bible names and numbering, with LXX differences noted where relevant.
