Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 313–386) was the bishop of Jerusalem in the fourth century. He wrote this homily to explain the saying of Christ recorded in John 14:12b–13, in which Christ declares that he is going to his Father and promises to do whatever is asked in his name so that the Father may be glorified in the Son.

The homily takes up the question of how the same pronoun "I" can refer to both Christ's divinity and his humanity. Cyril argues that the context shows the meaning of the words, while the difference in what is said distinguishes the two natures. Because Christ speaks as one person in both cases, the text points to the union of his person.

To follow the argument a reader should know that "divinity" and "humanity" here mean the two natures of Christ, "natures" refers to those two distinct realities united in one person, "person" is the single subject who speaks and acts, "union" is the joining of the natures in that one person, and "I" is the pronoun Christ uses of himself in the Gospel saying.
