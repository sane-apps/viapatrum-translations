Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) is the writer of these pieces. No addressee is named in what survives. They belong to his work explaining the New Testament.

What survives here are excerpts from his writings on John, Matthew and Luke, kept in later catena and scholia collections. They preserve his Gospel comments as later readers copied them.

He explains why the Son is called Word as maker of all things and called flesh as man for human salvation, defends that the Word always was, answers how John's saying that no one receives Jesus' testimony agrees with some receiving it, sets birth apart from creation in Matthew, and holds the four Gospels as the approved accounts against many attempts. The reader meets Jesus, John the Evangelist, John the Baptist, Luke, Matthew, Mark, Simon Peter, Job and Basilides, hears Scripture quoted throughout, and hears opponents answered where they are marked by such words as some think and they say. Word means the Son as maker and revealer, Wisdom names the Son with God in creation, flesh names his human state, testimony is his speech about the Father and himself, beginning of God's ways is Proverbs language for Wisdom, rebuke renders Jesus' speech to the fever, discernment of spirits is the gift for telling true from false prophets, and eyewitnesses and servants of the Word name those who saw his ministry and served it. The first of Kingdoms is 1 Samuel, Jesus son of Nave is Joshua, Old Testament citations follow the Septuagint, and Basilides is the heretic who dared to write a Gospel under his own name.
