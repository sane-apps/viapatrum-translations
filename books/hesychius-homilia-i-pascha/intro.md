Hesychius of Jerusalem (fl. c. 412–450) wrote this homily as a presbyter of Jerusalem for a congregation of faithful Christians gathered for the Paschal vigil in Jerusalem. He preached during the Paschal night vigil to celebrate Christ's resurrection, to proclaim his victory over death and the devil, and to invite the faithful to share in that triumph through veneration of the cross.

It belongs to the Easter vigil in Jerusalem and celebrates the risen Christ as conqueror of the world and the invisible enemy. It presents the Word hidden under the Law as under a bushel and revealed on the cross as on a lampstand, reads the rod of Moses, the drowning of Pharaoh, and the outstretched hands of Adam as types fulfilled in the cross and resurrection, holds up the good thief as the model of faith, and ends with Christ ascending to present the redeemed to the Father. The reader meets Jesus Christ, Adam and Eve, Pharaoh, Israel and the Egyptians, Paul who boasted in the cross, the good thief, the Father, Hades and the devil, and Rome and Jerusalem honoring the cross, and hears quoted words from Jesus, the good thief, the Day personified, and the Father in Psalm 109:1 LXX, while the view of the cross as a stumbling block for Jews is set against its reception as wisdom by Gentiles.

To follow it, read Easter for the Christian Passover feast of Christ's passion and resurrection, the Word for the incarnate Son, the Law for the Mosaic Law, lampstand for the cross raised for all to see, type for an Old Testament event fulfilled in Christ, Hades for the realm of the dead, paradise for the garden reopened by the cross, and victory and good news for Christ's conquest and its proclamation. Old Testament citations follow the Septuagint, the Greek Old Testament called LXX, so this translation uses standard English book names and gives psalm numbers in modern form with the LXX number noted, as in Psalm 24:7 (LXX 23:7).
