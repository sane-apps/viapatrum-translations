Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) is named as the author of this appendix and catalogue. No individual addressee is named for it. It was put together as a supplementary collection of traditions about apostles, disciples, deacons, and the origins of the four Gospels, to be appended to earlier indexes of apostles and disciples.

It records where figures such as Timothy, Titus, Crescens and the Ethiopian eunuch preached, died and were buried, then lists the seventy apostles and the seven deacons led by Stephen as first martyr. It gives the dates and circumstances for the composition of Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, and explains each Gospel by its opening theme as a symbol of one of the four living creatures, John as eagle, Luke as calf, Matthew as man and Mark as lion. It closes by noting that Mark and Luke were among the seventy who scattered over the hard saying about eating Christ's flesh and drinking his blood, but later returned to the Lord, the one through Peter and the other through Paul, and were deemed worthy to preach the Gospel. Clement is quoted as the source for the list of the seventy from the fifth book of the Outlines, Paul is mentioned as remembering Luke in a certain letter, and the Lord is quoted speaking the hard saying.

To follow it a reader should know that gospel here means both the message of the Lord Jesus Christ and the written books, that the seventy means a group of apostles distinct from the Twelve, that deacons means the seven appointed men, and that symbol means the likeness of each Gospel to a living creature. Outlines is the title of the work of Clement cited for the list, John is the one who interpreted Matthew's Gospel written in Hebrew, and the division of Abijah is the priestly division of Zechariah named at the opening of Luke. Old Testament references use Septuagint book names and Psalm numbering, Gospel and Pauline passages use modern names, and Taprobane is rendered as Sri Lanka.
