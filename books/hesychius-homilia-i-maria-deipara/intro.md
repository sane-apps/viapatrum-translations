Hesychius of Jerusalem (fl. c. 412–450) was a priest of the Church of Jerusalem.

He composed this festal homily for a congregation honoring Mary the Mother of God, celebrating her virgin motherhood and its role in the incarnation of the only-begotten Son. It belongs to a feast of the Virgin as Mother of God on August 15, shortly after the Council of Ephesus proclaimed her as Mother of God.

Hesychius praises Mary as the fitting instrument of the Word's flesh who bore the Creator with the seal of her womb unbroken, reads a psalm of David as addressed to Christ with Mary as the ark of sanctification, applies Isaiah's Emmanuel prophecy to her, dramatizes her questioning and Gabriel's reply, and ends with Christ's poverty and the manger as food leading to communion with the Trinity. The reader meets Mary, Gabriel who announces the conception, Christ the only-begotten Son and Word, Isaiah who foretold the virgin birth, David the psalmist, and Micah named for foretelling the place; the quoted words are David's psalm, Isaiah's prophecy, and Mary's question and Gabriel's answer from Luke, each expanded with explanation. To follow it, know that Mother of God renders her title as the one who gave birth to God the Word incarnate, with ever-virgin, seal for the unbroken womb, ark and tabernacle for her bearing God, closed gate from Ezekiel, enclosed garden and sealed fountain for virginity, Emmanuel meaning God with us, plan of salvation for God's saving act in the incarnation, and corruption for loss of virginity; Bible quotations follow the old Greek Septuagint whose psalm numbering differs from modern Bibles, the Songs means Song of Songs, and the Council of Ephesus was the church gathering whose decree affirmed her title.
