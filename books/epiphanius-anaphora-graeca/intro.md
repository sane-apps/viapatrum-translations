Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis in Cyprus and Metropolitan of the Church of Cyprus. Born in Judea, he practiced monasticism in Egypt and served as bishop for nearly forty years. He is best known for composing the Panarion, a compendium of eighty heresies.

This work is a eucharistic prayer attributed to Epiphanius. It gives thanks for God's benefits and recounts the institution of the Lord's Supper, and it asks that the bread and the cup may become the body and blood of Christ. Its language includes the words of institution for the bread and wine.

The prayer is addressed to God the Father and centers on Christ, the eternal Son, with his holy disciples receiving the bread and cup. Christ alone is quoted, in two sayings marked by saying, one for the bread as his body and one for the cup as his blood of the new covenant for the forgiveness of sins. Here the divine Word means the eternal Son who became flesh, sharing your throne speaks of his equal rule with the Father, co-eternal means without beginning, and without confusion or division means one Christ in deity and humanity. The cup of immortality means the cup that gives eternal life, mysteries means the eucharistic elements and the sacred rite, newness of spirit means renewed spiritual life, and changed renders only the petition that the bread may become the body of Christ. Biblical allusions follow standard English references such as Matthew, Mark, Luke, 1 Corinthians and John.
