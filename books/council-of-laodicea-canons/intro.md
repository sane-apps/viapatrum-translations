This work was issued by the Council of Laodicea (between 343 and 381) as disciplinary and liturgical canons. No addressee is named and no introduction states to whom they were sent or why they were issued.

They regulate admission to communion for the twice married and for penitent sinners, baptism and ordination, worship and Lent, marriage, fasting and separation from heresy and from Jewish and pagan festivals, and they close with a list of canonical books. The reader will meet bishops, presbyters, deacons, servants, readers, singers, exorcists, doorkeepers, ascetics, hearers, catechumens, penitents and the faithful, with Novatians, Photinians, Quartodecimans, Phrygians, Jews and pagans as outsiders to be avoided or received under rule. Only the council speaks in its own decrees, so no opponent is quoted in his own words.

Communion here means sharing in the Eucharist and in church fellowship, repentance means the process for readmission, excommunicated means excluded from communion for a time, sanctuary means the altar area for clergy only, and love feast means a communal church meal. Circuit visitors are appointees for rural areas instead of resident bishops, and deacon's stole renders the deacon's vestment. Old Testament names follow Greek use, so Jesus son of Nave means Joshua, Kingdoms means Samuel and Kings, Paralipomenon means Chronicles, Esdras means Ezra and Nehemiah, and the Twelve Prophets means the Minor Prophets.
