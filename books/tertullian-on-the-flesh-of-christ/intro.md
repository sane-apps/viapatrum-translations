Tertullian (c. 155–c. 220) wrote On the Flesh of Christ as a treatise for Christians troubled by heretical teachings about Christ's body.

He writes to refute Marcion, Apelles, Valentinus and others who denied that Christ had true flesh born from the Virgin Mary, because that denial undermines the Christian hope of the resurrection of the flesh.

He first defends the incarnation against Marcion's claim that birth and flesh are unworthy of God, then argues from Scripture and reason that Christ's flesh was true flesh from Mary's womb and not a phantom, and not starry, made of soul, spiritual or angelic, and that though of the same nature as ours it was without sin, so Christ's resurrection pledges ours. A reader will meet Christ, Mary, Marcion, Apelles with Philumene, Valentinus, Alexander and Ebion, with Abraham, David, Adam, Eve, Gabriel, Paul, Peter, Matthew, John, Simeon, Elizabeth, Joseph and Lazarus, and will hear Marcion, the followers of Apelles and the Valentinians quoted and answered, alongside Christ, Paul, Isaiah, David, John and Simeon quoted from Scripture. Here flesh means true body, birth means true birth, womb means Mary's womb, seed means seed, Word means God the Son, Son of Man names his true humanity and Son of God his divinity, while phantom means unreal appearance, starry means made of stars and made of soul means composed of soul stuff, and Jesse is the father of David whose root bears Christ as flower. Esaias is Isaiah, Hieremias is Jeremiah, Ezechiel is Ezekiel, Zacharias is Zechariah, Psalms follow a numbering one behind the Hebrew for much of the book, and Acta Apostolorum is the Acts of the Apostles.
