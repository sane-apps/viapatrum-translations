Hesychius of Jerusalem (fl. c. 412–450) was a priest in the church in Jerusalem and an interpreter of Scripture. This homily names no addressee and speaks to his congregation. He preached it for the feast of Easter to proclaim the resurrection of Christ and its cosmic significance.

It was delivered for Easter as a festal proclamation. It opens with a holy royal trumpet that has gathered a spiritual theater for the feast, which Bethlehem filled and Zion fired, with the Cross as hammer and the Resurrection as anvil.

Hesychius argues that the one incarnate Word truly suffers in the flesh and works miracles in his divinity, descends into Hades as dead and frees the dead as God, and fulfills Scripture when Adam is raised and the form of a servant is lifted to heaven. The reader meets Christ, Joseph who buried him, soldiers guarding the tomb, angels in shining garments who announce the resurrection to the weeping women, the cowering gatekeepers of Hades, Adam, and the prophets Hosea and Isaiah, and hears quoted the angel at the tomb, Hosea on the third day, Isaiah on the Lord rising, and the Lord speaking in Isaiah, while the Jews addressed in Isaiah are put to shame. Here Easter renders Pascha, the incarnate Word means the divine Word Christ, Hades means the realm of the dead, impassibility means freedom from suffering and death, types mean Old Testament shadows that end, and truth means their fulfillment in Christ. The Old Testament follows the Greek Septuagint, cited as Hosea 6:1-2 and Isaiah 33:9-10, with Jesus of Nazareth for Jesus the Nazarene.
