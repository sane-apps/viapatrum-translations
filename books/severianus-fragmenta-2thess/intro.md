Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) wrote for Christian readers, especially those troubled by persecution and speculation about Christ's return. He comments on the Second Letter to the Thessalonians to console believers suffering persecution and to correct mistaken expectations about the timing and manner of Christ's second coming, especially fears stirred by false prophecy and forged letters.

Paul writes to comfort the Thessalonians in persecution by reminding them of God's just recompense, punishment for the wicked and reward for the saints, and by teaching that the rebellion and the man of lawlessness must come first. Severian explains that the devil has long prepared idolatry and heresy as scaffolding for an arch for his own worship, and that Holy Spirit as restrainer holds back the antichrist until the appointed time, when Christ in his coming and appearing will destroy him by the breath of his mouth. The reader will meet Paul, whose letter is quoted throughout with phrases such as he says, Isaiah and the last of the prophets, who is Malachi, who are quoted on judgment, Elijah the Tishbite named in Malachi, and Mani and Marcion cited as early examples of the mystery of lawlessness opposed here, with the devil acting in the antichrist through the working of Satan.

To follow the argument it helps to know how key words are used here. Rebellion means the falling away before the day of the Lord. Man of lawlessness and antichrist name the deceiver to come. Restrainer names Holy Spirit holding him back. Coming names Christ's return and its counterfeit, while appearing names the manifestation that destroys the deceiver. Mystery of lawlessness names hidden evil already at work, and working of Satan names its power. The last of the prophets means Malachi. Bible quotations follow standard English names and numbering, with notes where the Greek version differs.
