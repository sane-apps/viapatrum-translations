Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) is the author to whom modern scholarship attributes this treatise, while the manuscript tradition attaches it to the name Nilus of Ancyra.

It was written for a monastic superior or spiritual guide, to show how to guide disciples toward virtue and guard them from harm, with comparisons drawn from seafaring, shepherding, warfare, medicine and the desert journey of Israel.

The argument is that an unskilled guide destroys those in his care, like an unskilled pilot or shepherd, while the skilled leader acts like a general and physician who prevents wounds or heals them quickly, arming disciples, training them for combat, correcting them without anger, and leading them through discipline to the promised land where they live the blessed life of freedom from passions and receive joy for their labors. The reader hears only Evagrius speaking in direct address to the leader, with no other voices quoted or opposed. In this translation leader means the superior responsible for disciples and disciple means his student, promised land means the state of freedom from passions, and citations use modern English Bible book names and Hebrew Psalm numbering though the author used the Septuagint, with Elim given for Ailim.
