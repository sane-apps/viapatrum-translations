Gregory of Nazianzus (c. 329/330–390) wrote this homily for a delegation of Egyptian Christians visiting him. He addresses them as faithful and orthodox Christians, and he uses their visit to defend the full divinity of the Son and the Holy Spirit against Arian and Sabellian errors.

Gregory greets the Egyptians as fellow orthodox Christians and contrasts their worship of the Triune God with Egypt's old animal and idol cults. He states that there is one God in three, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, equal in divinity and distinct in persons, with the Son possessing all that the Father has except causation and the Spirit possessing all that the Son has except sonship. The reader will meet Joseph, David, John, Luke, Paul, Peter and Ananias and Sapphira, will hear words of Scripture from the Old Testament and the New Testament woven into Gregory's address, and will hear Gregory argue against Arian and Sabellian error and against a present opponent who denies the Spirit's divinity.

As rendered here, divinity means the divine nature shared by the three, Trinity means Father, Son and Holy Spirit in one God, person means a distinct person in God, being means the one divine essence, equal honor means the equal dignity of the three, and Word means the Son and Holy Spirit means the third person. Joseph can mean the Old Testament patriarch who stored grain in Egypt or his successor, and the new Peter is not the Apostle Peter but a contemporary bishop likely Peter of Alexandria. The animal and idol names point to Egypt's old cults, and Old Testament quotations follow the Septuagint, so Psalm numbers follow that Greek numbering while Bible books use standard English names.
