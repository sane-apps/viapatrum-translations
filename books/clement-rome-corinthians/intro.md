This letter is traditionally linked to Clement of Rome (fl. c. 96), though it is anonymous and speaks as the church of God sojourning in Rome writing to the church of God sojourning in Corinth. It was sent because a few rash and arrogant individuals had stirred up a disgraceful and unholy faction there, greatly blaspheming the renowned name of the Corinthians.

The letter rebukes envy and faction that led to the removal or transfer of well regarded elders from their honored ministry, and it calls for repentance, humility and submission with restoration of harmony. Peter and Paul appear as examples of endurance under envy, while Paul also appears as the writer of an earlier letter to Corinth that named Cephas and Apollos, and Claudius Ephebus, Valerius Biton and Fortunatus appear as messengers from Rome. Extended quotation gives the words of God, of Christ, of the Holy Spirit and of Scripture, and the case is pressed against the few individuals whose faction removed or transferred duly appointed elders.

Here faction means the divisive break in Corinth and envy means its root cause, while elders, bishops and deacons name appointed leaders and ministry names their work, with repentance, humility, harmony, love and submission carrying the appeal and rule naming the fixed pattern and the elect naming those God has called. Quotations follow the Greek Old Testament, so book names and Psalm numbers differ from English Bibles, and some passages called Scripture cannot be found in them. The All-Seeing is a name for God, and Claudius Ephebus, Valerius Biton and Fortunatus are the messengers who carry the letter.
