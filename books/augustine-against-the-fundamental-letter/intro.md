Augustine of Hippo (354–430) wrote this treatise for Manichaeans, and in part for Patticus, the correspondent addressed within Mani's letter. Born in Thagaste in Roman Africa and later bishop of Hippo Regius, Augustine had himself been a hearer among the Manichaeans for about nine years before becoming a Christian. He writes to refute the opening of Mani's Letter of Fundamental Principles, which Manichaeans treated as a foundational text, to show that its claims are false and that its promised certain knowledge is never delivered.

Augustine first explains why he remains in the Catholic Church, pointing to its authority, its universality and its succession from the apostles, which alone moved him to believe the gospel. He then examines the opening of the Fundamental Letter, arguing that Mani cannot prove himself an apostle of Christ or the promised Paraclete, that his promise of certain knowledge collapses into a demand for blind faith, and that his account of two eternal substances, the land of light and the land of darkness, is incoherent and portrays God as a bodily mass extended through space and penetrated by evil. The reader will meet Mani, Patticus, who asked about the birth of Adam and Eve, Paul, Theophilus, Adam and Eve. Mani's own words are quoted directly and argued against, alongside objections from an imagined Manichaean speaker and passages from Scripture, and Augustine closes by arguing that evil is not a nature but corruption, that all natures insofar as they exist are good, and that creatures were made from nothing.

To follow the discussion it helps to know how central words are used here. Substance means the underlying reality of a thing, nature means a created kind, and corruption means the loss of good in a changeable thing. The land of light and the land of darkness belong to Mani's account, the Paraclete is the Holy Spirit promised by Christ whom Mani claimed to be, from nothing means that God made creatures not from his own substance, and the Catholic Church means the universal Church. Augustine quotes the Old Testament from Latin versions based on the Septuagint and the New Testament from Old Latin versions, calls the book the Acts of the Apostles, and Psalm numbers follow the older Latin numbering. Hearers are the lower rank of Manichaean adherents, and the Bema festival is the Manichaean festival commemorating Mani's death.
