Hesychius of Jerusalem (fl. c. 412-450) was a Christian priest of the Church of Jerusalem, known as a theologian, biblical commentator and preacher in the Eastern Church who took part in the controversy on the nature of Christ.

He wrote this homily in Greek at Jerusalem as a festal encomium for the joint commemoration of the apostles Peter and Paul, preached at their feast on 28 December to praise their complementary roles and to tell Paul's change from persecutor to apostle.

The homily first praises Peter as fisherman, shepherd and keeper of the keys, then joins him with Paul in one pair, and centers on Christ speaking from heaven to Saul on the road to Damascus, with Saul answering with Who are you, Lord, while Paul's belief without disputing is set against heretics who question. Readers meet Peter, Paul as Saul, Christ as the eternal Son, and David as the psalmist set against Saul. In this rendering Law means the Law of Moses, grace is added to it, a type is an Old Testament figure pointing to Christ, and pair means Peter and Paul together; quotations follow the Septuagint, so Psalm numbers and book forms differ, and modern references use Proverbs, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Zechariah, Daniel, Jonah, Ezekiel, Hebrews and 2 Corinthians.
