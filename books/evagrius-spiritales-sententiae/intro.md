This work was written by Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399). No single addressee is named. It was prepared for monastic formation and moral instruction.

It is a collection of brief spiritual maxims arranged by initial letter for monastic life. It teaches that salvation begins with self-condemnation, that virtue must be practiced in deeds rather than words, and that the soul must be purified from passions, contrasting true piety, friendship and wisdom with vainglory, pride and love of pleasure, and closing with knowledge of God, humility and union with Christ through keeping his commandments. The only figure a reader meets is Jesus Christ, presented as the tree of life, the one whose body is eaten and whose commandments are kept. No other voices are quoted or opposed, and after his death his teachings were judged unorthodox in some later church circles, although his works continued to be cherished in the Armenian and Syrian churches.

To follow it a reader needs the renderings used here. Virtue means excellence of soul practiced in deed, soul means the inner person, and passions means disordered desires, with vainglory, pride, self-love, humility, contemplation, almsgiving, commandments and Scriptures used in the senses given in this translation. The text speaks of the Scriptures without citing particular books, so no old book names appear. Where self-control and gentleness of soul are called cherubim, cherubim means angelic beings used as symbols for those virtues.
