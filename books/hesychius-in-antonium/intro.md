Hesychius of Jerusalem (fl. c. 412–450), a priest of Jerusalem, addressed this homily to a congregation assembled for the feast of Saint Antony. He spoke on the commemoration of Antony to mark his commemoration and to urge his hearers to imitate his virtues.

It is a festal encomium delivered at the church of the Anastasis in Jerusalem on 17 January for Antony the Egyptian desert monk. Hesychius declares that the commemoration of the righteous enlightens the world and presents Antony as a citizen of heaven, then praises him through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, David, Daniel, John the Baptist, John the Evangelist and Paul, with Elijah and Christ also named. The words of Paul from Hebrews, David from the Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Job, Solomon from Proverbs and Christ from Matthew are all quoted in praise of Antony, and none are argued against.

A reader needs only a few renderings to follow it. Commemoration means the liturgical feast, the righteous means those counted righteous, virtue means holy conduct, ascetic life means the monastic way, way of life means communal conduct, and offspring means spiritual descendants in monastic communities. Anastasis means Resurrection and names the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, LXX means the Greek Septuagint whose Psalm numbers differ from the Hebrew, Hebrews is quoted as Paul's, John the Theologian means John the Evangelist, learning from without means secular education and learning from within means Christian teaching, and the Sun of Righteousness means Christ.
