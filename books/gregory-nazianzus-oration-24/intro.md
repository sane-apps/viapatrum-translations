Gregory of Nazianzus (c. 329/330–390) wrote this panegyric oration.

It is addressed to a congregation assembled for the annual commemoration of the martyr Cyprian, which especially venerates him. Gregory had returned from the countryside one day after the commemoration and delivered this delayed encomium.

Gregory recounts how Cyprian, once a demon worshipper, sorcerer and persecutor, was converted after a Christian virgin defeated a demon sent to seduce her, then burned his magical books, became bishop, encouraged martyrs by letter from exile under Decius, was beheaded, and his hidden body was later revealed by God through revelation to a worthy woman for the common good. The reader meets the virgin and Mary as helper, Decius, Paul and Matthew as converted sinners, Susanna and Thecla as rescued women, Joseph, Job, Saul afflicted by an evil spirit, Beliar as a name for the devil, and quoted words of David, Solomon and Christ. Here martyr, contest, athlete, crown, conversion, sorcery, self-control, philosophy and way of life keep these senses, hieromartyr means a bishop martyr, and Psalms follow the Greek Septuagint numbering, which differs in places from the Hebrew.
