Eustathius of Antioch (fl. c. 320–337) wrote this homily for a Christian congregation whom he addresses as beloved. He preaches on the Gospel account of the supper at Bethany to proclaim Christ as divine and to defend the full reality of his incarnation against Jewish objections.

The occasion is a festal homily on the supper at Bethany, where the risen Lazarus reclines with Jesus while Martha and Mary serve him. The preacher treats the presence of Lazarus at table as proof that his resurrection was real and not a phantom, and he uses the scene to teach that Christ is the eternal Son of God and not a mere anointed man.

The homily moves from John and the supper to the fire of the knowledge of God and the confession of Martha set beside those of Peter and John, then to Mary anointing Jesus and finally to an answer to a Jewish objection and a doxology to the Son consubstantial with the Father and the Holy Spirit. The reader will meet Lazarus, Martha, Mary, John who is called son of thunder, Peter, Judas, Jacob, Moses, Isaiah, Solomon, Elijah, Job, Lot and Esau. John, Jesus, Martha, Peter, Jacob, Matthew and Solomon are quoted directly while Isaiah is given as an adapted allusion, and the words of a blind Jew are quoted to be answered. Here fire means the life giving fire of the knowledge of God, sign means a miracle that leads to faith, confession means confession of Christ as Son of God, and unchanged, without confusion and without division describe the union of divine and human in Christ. The Old Testament is cited in its Greek Septuagint forms, so Psalm numbering follows the Septuagint and modern citations use standard English Bible book names.
