Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) wrote this treatise for one who has been deemed worthy of knowledge. The reader addressed is an advanced Christian ascetic who has been granted spiritual knowledge. The work was written to guide him in conduct, teaching, and contemplation.

Evagrius distinguishes the practical ascetic, who has calmed the passionate part of the soul, from the gnostic, who has received spiritual knowledge by God's grace and must guard it through virtue, especially freedom from anger. The gnostic must avoid lawsuits, love of money, harshness, and careless speech about divine things, while discerning when and to whom to speak, and he must train his mind against false thoughts and learn from approved teachers. A reader will meet Gregory the Theologian, Basil of Caesarea, Athanasius of Alexandria, Sarapion the bishop of Thmuis, and Didymus the Blind, whose words are quoted and reported, and also Balaam and Caiaphas, blameworthy persons whose prophetic words about the Savior were used through divine arrangement, and Abenner, a Levite who received the ark and became rich and honored.

As rendered here, gnostic means the advanced Christian who has received spiritual knowledge, and practical ascetic means one who has attained freedom from passion but not yet that knowledge. Freedom from anger is the virtue most needed to guard knowledge, mind is the faculty that receives it, contemplation is its activity, principles are the inner meanings of created things, original pattern is the model the gnostic looks to, accommodation is adjustment to others without making it habit, divine arrangement is God's special use even of blameworthy speakers, and secret teachings are those not to be shared for gain or passing glory. The author follows the Septuagint, so Bible books appear under their Greek names, and the ark story is cited as 1 Samuel, where the name Abenner is a variant form for Abinadab. Angel of the church means bishop, so Sarapion is the bishop of the people of Thmuis, the just Gregory is Gregory the Theologian, and the irascible part is the anger prone part of the soul.
