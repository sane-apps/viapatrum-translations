Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) wrote this paraphrase of Ecclesiastes for the whole Church of God, to present Solomon's teaching on the vanity of human affairs for the benefit of the Church.

He presents the teaching as Solomon's, reported under the mark Thus says Solomon. The reader will meet Solomon, son of King David and king of the Hebrews in Jerusalem, as the source of the teaching, and David as father of Solomon. Solomon declares that all human affairs and pursuits are empty and useless, that human life wears away like winter torrents pouring into the boundless deep, that what God has established remains fixed while human words and deeds have no measure, and that nothing new ever happens, since what seems new was already known to the ancients.

To follow it, a reader needs only a few renderings used here. Empty and useless describes human affairs, pursuits are earnest human endeavors, and idle talk is talk that brings no benefit. The human race is shown as insatiable in speaking and listening, winter torrents picture how quickly life passes, and the noble eye of the soul is the power to look above the stars. The book paraphrased is cited by its Septuagint name as Ecclesiastes (LXX).
