Hesychius of Jerusalem (fl. c. 412–450) wrote this homily for a congregation in Jerusalem for the feast of the Presentation of Christ in the Temple. He expounds Luke's account of Simeon and Anna and says time is short, so he offers a condensed explanation suited to the occasion and to the hearers' desire.

Hesychius shows how the Holy Spirit made Simeon a prophet and temple of God and led him to receive the infant Christ, reads Simeon's song as thanksgiving for the Savior prepared for all peoples, and explains Christ as set for the fall and rising of many and as a sign contradicted in the cross, before presenting Anna as prophetess, widow and temple dweller proclaiming the redemption of Jerusalem to all awaiting it. The reader meets Simeon, Anna, Mary, Joseph, Jesus, Phanuel, David, Cleopas, Mary Magdalene, Peter and Gabriel. Simeon, Peter, Cleopas and Mary Magdalene are quoted from Scripture, Anna's proclamation and the Spirit's address to Simeon are reconstructed, and Luke is shown answering an objection about calling Joseph father in order to deceive the enemy opposed to salvation.

Here Presentation means the meeting of the Lord in the temple, consolation means the comfort of Israel, salvation means God's saving work seen in Christ, revelation means Christ as light to the nations, and sign contradicted, sword, thoughts and redemption follow the cross and Anna's message. Names follow this form with Simeon for Symeon and Mary for Mariam, Bible books use Septuagint names, and Septuagint Psalm numbers run one behind modern numbering so the eighty fourth psalm appears as eighty five in modern Bibles. Cleopas is the disciple met on the road to Emmaus, Phanuel is Anna's father and Asher is her tribe, and unusual passages give the Spirit's presumed words to Simeon, Luke's reply to an objector, and a reconstruction of what Anna was saying.
