Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was the Christian bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, born to a pagan family under the name Theodore and converted after studying with Origen at Caesarea in Palestine, and ordained by Phaedimus of Amasea. This homily names no individual addressee and gives no date of composition. It was written to be spoken to the faithful as a festal sermon.

The occasion is the day of the Annunciation, when Gabriel came to the Virgin Mary and announced the conception of Jesus Christ. The homily proclaims that the mystery hidden from eternity is now made known and that God gives crowns to the faithful, links the joy of the feast to David's oracle that heaven and earth rejoice at the Lord's coming and to Christ's promise of lasting joy, and then recounts Gabriel's greeting of full of grace, Mary's question, and the answer that the Holy Spirit will come upon her and the child will be called Son of God. The reader meets Gabriel, Mary called Maria and Virgin in the source, Jesus Christ, David, and the Forerunner who calls for trees producing fruits worthy of repentance. David, Jesus Christ, Gabriel and Mary are quoted directly, and no opposing voice is argued against here.

To follow it, read mystery for the divine secret now revealed, crowns for reward and honor, heirs for the faithful, joy for lasting joy, full of grace for Gabriel's greeting, Holy Spirit, power of the Most High, Son of God and Son of the Most High for the child, and angelic choirs for the heavenly ministry. The Psalm quotation follows the Septuagint numbering with David named as speaker, where Psalm 95 corresponds to Psalm 96 in Hebrew numbering, and the Annunciation dialogue follows Luke 1:28-35. The Forerunner is not explicitly named as John in the text.
